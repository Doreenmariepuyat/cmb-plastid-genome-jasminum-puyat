
## 1. What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?

The scientific name is *Jasminum nudiflorum*. Its plastid genome accession number is NC_008407.1.

## 2. What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?

The total length of the plastid genome is 165,121 base pairs (bp).

## 3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.

Yes. The map shows four major regions. The LSC (Large Single-Copy) is the largest region and occupies most of the upper part of the map. The SSC (Small Single-Copy) is the smaller region located at the lower part of the map. The IRa (Inverted Repeat A) is located between the LSC and SSC on the right side. And the IRb (Inverted Repeat B) is located between the SSC and LSC on the left side. The published genome organization gives the LSC as 92,877 bp, SSC as 13,272 bp, and each IR as 29,486 bp.

## 4. Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.

Three examples are: psbA, rbcL, rpoA. These genes are visible in the LSC portion of the map.

## 5. Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.

One example is ndhA. It is located in the SSC region and is involved in the photosynthetic electron transport system.

## 6. Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?

One example is rpl2, and yes, as genes located within an inverted repeat region can occur twice because the chloroplast genome has two similar IR regions, IRa and IRb. The map shows duplicated copies of several genes in these regions.

## 7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.

One example is psbA. The psbA gene codes for the Photosystem II D1 protein, which is important for photosynthesis and the light-dependent reactions.

## 8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?

The arrows show the direction in which the genes are transcribed. They indicate which direction the DNA information is read to produce RNA.

## 9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions?

The GC content is not completely uniform across the genome. The graph shows small variations in GC content between different regions. However, the overall pattern does not show extremely large differences.

## 10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?

A graphical map makes the genome much easier to understand and visualize. It allows us to quickly see the LSC, SSC, IR regions, gene locations, gene directions, and other genome features. A raw DNA sequence or a long annotation list contains the information but is harder to understand at a glance.
